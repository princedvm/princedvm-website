Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) remains one of the most economically damaging livestock diseases in Bangladesh. While it rarely kills adult cattle, it sharply reduces milk yield, draft power and body condition — losses that hit smallholder families hardest.

Know the signs

  • High fever followed by blisters on the tongue, gums, muzzle, teats and between the hooves
  • Excessive drooling and smacking of the lips
  • Lameness and reluctance to stand
  • Sudden drop in milk production

Five prevention steps every farmer can take

  1. Vaccinate on schedule. Bring your cattle to the free government vaccination campaigns announced by the Upazila Livestock Office.
  2. Quarantine new animals for at least two weeks before mixing them with your herd.
  3. Disinfect shoes, vehicles and equipment that move between farms and markets.
  4. Avoid shared grazing and communal watering points during an outbreak.
  5. Report suspected cases immediately to the Upazila Livestock Office so we can respond before the disease spreads.

If you suspect FMD in your herd, do not panic and do not sell the animal at market — call the Upazila Livestock Office, Baliakandi. Early reporting protects your neighbours' animals as well as your own.
